A growing body of work shows that sleep contributes to memory consolidation (Rasch & Born, 2013).
These effects may be modest, and they appear to depend on sleep stage, particularly slow-wave
sleep, though the mechanisms are not fully understood (Diekelmann & Born, 2010). In this study, we
use a within-subjects design to test the hypothesis that targeted memory reactivation during
slow-wave sleep enhances retention relative to a wake control condition.
