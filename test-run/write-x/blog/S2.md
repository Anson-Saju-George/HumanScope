# Why we moved from Jira to Linear

Last quarter our engineering team, all twelve of us, moved our work out of Jira and into Linear. People have asked how it went, and it's early, so here's where we've landed so far: what pushed us to switch, what got better, and what we gave up.

## What wasn't working

Jira was slow. The problem wasn't any single page load. It was all the small waits: opening a ticket, changing a field, moving between boards. Each one was minor, but they added up over a day, and the tool began to feel like something we had to get through before we could do the work.

The bigger cost was grooming. We spent too long on the tickets themselves: tidying fields, re-sorting the backlog, going over the same items meeting after meeting. For a team our size, the process had grown heavier than the work it was supposed to organize. When the tracker takes more care than the code, something is off.

## What Linear gave us

**It's keyboard-first.** Almost everything in Linear can be done without touching the mouse: creating an issue, assigning it, changing its status, jumping to another view. That sounds like a small thing. In practice it means updating a ticket takes a few keystrokes, so people actually keep their tickets up to date. The tracker stays accurate because keeping it accurate costs almost nothing.

**Cycles match how we already worked.** We were already working in short, fixed-length iterations. Linear's cycles are built around that rhythm: a set time window, the work pulled into it, and anything unfinished rolling into the next one. We didn't have to bend our process to fit the tool, or build that structure ourselves on top of it.

Together, these changes made the tracker feel lighter. We spend less time managing tickets and more time on the work they describe.

## Would we do it again?

For a twelve-person team that wants to spend its time building, yes. The speed and the lighter process have been worth more to us than the reports we gave up. We're still early, though, and still working out how to replace the parts of Jira we miss. We'll share more once we've had a few more cycles to judge.
