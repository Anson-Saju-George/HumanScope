# Why We Moved From Jira to Linear

Last quarter our engineering team, all twelve of us, stopped using Jira and moved to Linear. We don't think Jira is a bad product. Plenty of teams, some much larger than ours, run on it happily. It had just stopped fitting the way we work, and we were paying for that every day in small ways.

Here's why we switched, what got better, and what we gave up.

## The tool was slow, and we could feel it

The first complaint was speed, and it came up constantly. Opening a ticket, changing its status, jumping to a linked issue, filtering a board: each of these took long enough to notice. A second or two doesn't sound like much. But an engineer touches the tracker dozens of times a day, and those pauses add up to a steady low-level irritation. People started avoiding the tracker. They'd update tickets in batches at the end of the day, or not at all, and the board drifted away from reality.

A tracker only helps if it reflects what's actually happening. Ours increasingly didn't.

## We spent too much time grooming tickets

The second problem was grooming. Over the years our Jira setup had collected custom fields, required fields, several issue types, and workflows with more states than anyone could remember. Each one was added for a good reason at the time. Together they turned backlog grooming into a long meeting where we were mostly tidying up metadata instead of deciding what to build.

When grooming feels like paperwork, people put it off, the backlog grows, and the next session takes even longer. We were stuck in that cycle.

## Why Linear fit

We looked at a few options. Linear won for two reasons.

**It's keyboard-first.** Almost everything in Linear has a shortcut: creating an issue, assigning it, changing its status, moving it into a cycle, searching. Once you learn a handful of keys, you stop reaching for the mouse, and updating the tracker takes seconds instead of a context switch. Most of our engineers already live in their editor and terminal, so this felt natural within the first week. It's also fast. Pages load quickly and actions register right away, which on its own fixed a lot of the "I'll update it later" habit.

**Cycles match how we already plan.** We work in short, fixed iterations. Linear's cycles are built around exactly that: a time-boxed chunk of work, with unfinished issues rolling over automatically. We didn't have to bend a general-purpose sprint setup into shape or maintain a custom workflow to get there. The defaults were close to what we wanted, so we kept our configuration light, and we've been deliberate about not rebuilding the field sprawl we left behind.

Grooming is shorter now. Issues carry less required metadata, triage is quick, and our planning conversations are about priorities again rather than cleanup.

## What we lost: reporting

The switch had a real cost. Jira's reporting was more mature than what we have now, and some of it we relied on. Over time we had built dashboards and reports that leadership and a few people outside engineering looked at regularly. Linear has its own insights and project views, and they cover a lot, but they don't reproduce everything we had. Some reports we couldn't recreate as they were.

We've handled it in a few ways:

- **We asked which reports people actually used.** Some turned out to be looked at rarely, and we let those go.
- **For the ones that mattered, we rebuilt what we could** using Linear's built-in views and, where needed, by pulling data out through its API.
- **We accepted a gap for now.** A few views we used to get for free now take manual work, or don't exist yet. We knew that going in and decided the daily speed gain for the whole team was worth it.

If your organization depends heavily on detailed, cross-team reporting out of the tracker, take this seriously before you switch. Make a list of the reports you need and check each one against Linear before you commit.

## Would we do it again?

Yes. For a team our size, what matters most is that the tool stays out of the way and that the board shows what's really going on. Linear does both better for us than our Jira setup did. We traded some reporting depth for a tracker people actually keep up to date, and so far that's been the right call.

Our advice if you're considering a similar move: be honest about which pain you're trying to fix. For us it was friction and overhead in day-to-day work. If your main pain is visibility for people outside engineering, the answer might look different.
