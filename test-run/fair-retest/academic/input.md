Recent work has increasingly demonstrated that sleep plays a pivotal and crucial role in memory
consolidation (Rasch & Born, 2013). It is important to note that these effects may be modest and
appear to depend on sleep stage, particularly slow-wave sleep, though the precise mechanisms remain
incompletely understood (Diekelmann & Born, 2010). In this study, we leverage a within-subjects
design to test the hypothesis that targeted memory reactivation during slow-wave sleep enhances
retention relative to a wake control condition.
