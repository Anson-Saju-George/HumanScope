The kettle was still warm when Dana got to her mother's kitchen, which meant Ruth had left in a hurry. A mug sat on the counter, the tea bag still in it, steeped dark and bitter. Dana poured it out, rinsed the mug, and set it upside down on the rack the way Ruth always did.

Her phone buzzed. "Don't come," Ruth had written. "I'm fine. I'll call tonight."

Dana read it twice. Then she opened the fridge, took out the eggs, and started cracking them into a bowl, one after another, until there were eight, more than anyone could eat.

When the door finally opened, Dana didn't turn around. "Sit down," she said. "It's almost ready."
