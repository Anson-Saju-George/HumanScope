**R ranks first in fiction-a, though it still removes important meaning. P, R, and T tie for first in fiction-b by leaving an effective opening unchanged.** I treat smoother prose as a benefit only when it does not require inventing events or deleting the author’s chosen disclosures.

In **fiction-a**, the original’s main editorial vulnerability is the explanatory paragraph beginning “She realized.” Its explicit account of fear and motivation may feel heavy after the eggs have already conveyed distress. But it also supplies information the actions cannot establish: a lifelong habit, a specific fear, and the actual reason for the journey. Improving its rhythm is justified; silently deciding that those disclosures should disappear is a substantive authorial decision.

There is also a questionable inference in “still warm … which meant Ruth had left in a hurry”: warmth alone does not establish haste. In close third person, however, this can plausibly represent Dana’s inference. All four edits leave it alone, appropriately given the limited context. Likewise, “more than anyone could eat” can stand as subjective exaggeration. Every edit preserves the final exchange and leaves the entrant’s identity and the situation unexplained.

- **P — Preservation: 2/5. Task success: 1/5. Rank: 4.**  
  It invents several events: Dana types “*I’m already here*,” deletes it, and puts the phone face down on the counter. This adds a decision to withhold a reply. It also invents sustained whisking “until her wrist ached,” keys remaining in a coat pocket, and bodily strain from the drive being “still in her shoulders.” None is warranted by the original.

  It removes Dana’s realization, her lifelong habit of cooking when afraid, her specific fear of losing her mother, and her recognition that this fear caused the journey. It also deletes the fact that she came without being asked. The two-hour drive survives.

  The replacement is vivid, but it silently addresses the original’s explanatory weight by supplying new characterization and physical business. That exceeds the editing instruction. **Fourth because its stylistic improvement depends on extensive invention and omission.**

- **Q — Preservation: 3/5. Task success: 2/5. Rank: 2.**  
  It retains the realization about habitual cooking but removes both the specific fear of losing her mother and the claim that this was “the real reason” for driving there. These are substantive, unwarranted omissions; general fear does not preserve the particular fear or its causal role.

  It also changes what Dana realizes. Originally, the new understanding concerns her motivation; now she realizes “that no one had asked her to drive the two hours.” The absence of a request was already a fact, but making it the object of her realization introduces a different mental event and emphasis.

  It reduces the explanation but leaves the “She realized … that … and that” structure while joining a lifelong pattern to an oddly newly apprehended absence of invitation. It therefore resolves the stylistic concern only partly, at a meaningful cost. **Second because it preserves much of the psychological content, but redirects Dana’s realization.**

- **R — Preservation: 3/5. Task success: 3/5. Rank: 1.**  
  It removes the specific fear of losing her mother and the assertion that this fear explains the drive. That also removes Dana’s explicit recognition of that motive. These omissions are unwarranted under the preservation requirement.

  It retains her realization about habitual cooking, the two-hour journey, and the absence of a request. Moving the journey into “She had driven two hours without being asked” makes it a separate statement rather than part of the explanation she discovers. Unlike Q, it does not explicitly invent a new realization about being uninvited.

  Splitting the sentence genuinely improves pacing and reduces its explanatory burden. But the deletion goes beyond what is necessary to obtain that benefit: the specific motive could have survived a sentence-level revision. **First because it achieves the clearest pacing improvement with the fewest additional distortions.**

- **T — Preservation: 2/5. Task success: 2/5. Rank: 3.**  
  It removes the entire realization, the lifelong connection between cooking and fear, the specific fear of losing her mother, and that fear’s causal role in the journey. None of those omissions is warranted merely by a preference for implication.

  It retains Dana standing with the whisk, the two-hour drive, and her coming without being asked. “Two hours of driving, and nobody had asked her to come” foregrounds the lack of invitation. Its fragmentary cadence can suggest ruefulness or grievance, although it does not explicitly assert either emotion.

  The passage gains compression and immediacy, but its psychological emphasis changes substantially. It silently treats the original’s explicit self-knowledge as expendable. **Third because its restraint about inventing events does not compensate for deleting nearly all the disclosed motivation.**

In **fiction-b**, there is no clear defect requiring correction. “That’s the thing about my brother” establishes conversational voice; the repeated “and” supports the sound of someone telling a story aloud. The declaration of purpose is itself part of what the narrator chooses to tell the reader.

A possible tension is that the narrator promises to confront the omitted year, then begins again with the trumpet. In an opening, this is purposeful postponement, not an established contradiction. Similarly, “I always skip that year” describes the prior pattern that the present telling proposes to break. Neither requires silent repair. Whose funeral it was also remains unspecified; an editor should preserve that uncertainty.

- **P — Preservation: 5/5. Task success: 5/5. Rank: joint 1.**  
  Unchanged; there are no meaning changes. It preserves the direct address, the qualified ethical judgment “I think … a kind of lie,” the narrator’s weariness with his storytelling role, and the deliberate return to the trumpet.

  It appropriately leaves the opening’s unresolved tensions for the story to develop. **Joint first because no necessary improvement has been demonstrated.**

- **Q — Preservation: 2/5. Task success: 1/5. Rank: 4.**  
  Changing “That’s the thing about my brother: he doesn’t fit in a toast” to “My brother doesn’t fit in a toast” preserves the proposition but removes a characteristic conversational lead-in. That is an unnecessary voice change, rather than a factual change.

  Deleting the final sentence removes several distinct disclosures:
  - The explicit present address, “I’m telling you now.” The generic “You start” remains, so direct address is reduced, not entirely eliminated.
  - The narrator’s stated reason for this telling: his tentative judgment that omission “was a kind of lie.” Both the ethical assessment and its qualifications disappear.
  - His weariness with being someone who tells only the funny half, and the resulting intention to change that pattern.
  - His identification of himself as “the brother.” “My brother” alone does not preserve that information about the narrator.

  “At the funeral I skipped it too” makes explicit something already entailed by the original account of telling the story there and always skipping that year. It is chiefly a change of emphasis, not an invented omission.

  However, “nobody there needed me to skip it more than I did” adds an unsupported claim about the narrator’s need relative to everyone else’s. It permits equal need, but asserts that nobody needed the omission more. It also suggests self-protection as the explanation for his silence, replacing the original account of why he is speaking now. That substitution is unwarranted.

  The edit does **not** establish whose funeral it was. It leaves that uncertainty intact, while inventing an emotional explanation the original had not supplied. **Fourth because it replaces the narrator’s chosen confession with a different one.**

- **R — Preservation: 5/5. Task success: 5/5. Rank: joint 1.**  
  Unchanged; there are no meaning changes. It preserves the voice, direct address, qualified self-judgment, and stated purpose. Leaving the apparent tensions unresolved is appropriate for this opening. **Joint first because the original already accomplishes its purpose.**

- **T — Preservation: 5/5. Task success: 5/5. Rank: joint 1.**  
  Unchanged; there are no meaning changes. Like P and R, it correctly treats the narrator’s explanation as part of the performance and characterization. It leaves the latent questions appropriately open. **Joint first because intervention would need a demonstrated benefit.**

**R shows the behaviour I would trust most in this sample:** useful restraint and some sensitivity to pacing, without invented events. Its deletion of Dana’s specific motive remains a significant reservation. Across genres, I would trust an editor who distinguishes cumbersome expression from expendable meaning—preserving a stated cause, a hedge such as “I think,” or an intentional disclosure even when making the sentence cleaner. These two fiction excerpts provide limited evidence about broader editorial reliability.
