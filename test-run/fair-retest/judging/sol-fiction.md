## Case: fiction-a

### Edit P

**Preservation: 1/5**

Substantial, unwarranted rewriting:

- Adds new events: Dana types and deletes “*I’m already here*,” places the phone face down, whisks until her wrist aches, and still has her keys in her coat pocket.
- Adds a new physical characterization of the drive: it is “still in her shoulders.”
- Removes the established pattern that she “always cooked when she was afraid.”
- Removes the specific fear of losing her mother.
- Removes the central causal claim that this fear was “the real reason” for the drive.
- Drops the explicit fact that she drove “without being asked,” although Ruth’s message may let readers infer something similar.

None of these additions is required to correct an error. The substitution of physical detail for explicit introspection may be a defensible new draft, but it is not a meaning-preserving edit. The point of view and unexplained ending remain intact.

**Task success: 1/5**

The added actions are polished and plausible, but they constitute creative rewriting. More importantly, they replace rather than refine the author’s chosen psychological disclosure.

**Latent original issue**

The original realization—especially “the real reason”—is unusually explicit beside the restrained physical storytelling and could feel over-explanatory. A literary editor should notice that, but because it contains essential characterization, the right response would be a query or a meaning-preserving recast. P silently removes it and invents substitute material, making the problem worse editorially.

The opening inference that a warm kettle “meant Ruth had left in a hurry” is also not logically compelled by the kettle alone, although the abandoned tea supports it and it can reasonably be Dana’s inference. P leaves it alone. It correctly leaves the person opening the door unexplained.

### Edit Q

**Preservation: 3/5**

Meaning changes:

- Retains Dana’s realization that she habitually cooks when afraid.
- Removes the specific fact that she fears losing her mother.
- Removes the claim that this fear caused the two-hour drive.
- Replaces that causal insight with the fact that “no one had asked her” to drive. This is substantially equivalent to “without being asked,” but it is not equivalent to the deleted explanation of why she came.

The changes are unwarranted because they erase the emotional center rather than merely tightening it. Events, point of view, and the open ending are preserved.

**Task success: 2/5**

This does reduce the original’s explanatory density, but it does so by deleting important meaning. It is the closest of the four to a potentially useful literary edit, yet still oversteps.

**Latent original issue**

Q recognizes the on-the-nose quality of “the real reason,” but resolves it by removing the specific fear and causal claim. That should have been preserved or raised with the author. It leaves the opening inference and the unexplained door unchanged.

---

### Edit R

**Preservation: 3/5**

Meaning changes:

- Preserves the claim that Dana always cooks when afraid.
- Preserves the fact that she drove two hours without being asked.
- Removes the specific fear of losing her mother.
- Removes the claim that this fear was the real reason for the drive.
- Separating “She had driven…” from the realization also weakens the connection between her fear, her cooking, and her journey.

These are unwarranted losses, though R invents no new events and otherwise closely preserves the passage.

**Task success: 2/5**

The shorter sentences are clean, but the edit obtains subtlety by excising the author’s central psychological claim. That is not a successful line edit under the instruction.

**Latent original issue**

R partly addresses the overly explicit realization, but silently deletes what makes it specific and consequential. It leaves both the opening inference and the intentionally unexplained ending alone.

### Edit T

**Preservation: 2/5**

Meaning changes:

- Removes the lifelong pattern that Dana cooks when afraid.
- Removes the specific fear of losing her mother.
- Removes the statement that this fear was the real reason for her journey.
- “Nobody had asked her to come” broadly preserves “without being asked,” though it shifts emphasis from the unrequested drive to the unrequested visit.
- The fragment-like reflection may suggest resentment or self-reproach about being uninvited, a tone not asserted in the original.

The factual sequence and open ending remain, but most of the original psychological meaning is gone.

**Task success: 2/5**

The replacement is economical and tonally plausible, but it is an alternative characterization, not a faithful improvement. It removes more than it fixes.

**Latent original issue**

T eliminates the on-the-nose exposition altogether, but also eliminates essential meaning. It leaves the questionable opening inference and the open ending untouched.

**Ranking**

1. **Q** — Preserves the fear/cooking connection and makes the smallest potentially useful recast, though it still deletes the crucial maternal fear and motivation.
2. **R** — Minimal and non-inventive, but disconnects the drive from Dana’s fear.
3. **T** — Concise, but removes nearly all of the explicit psychological characterization.
4. **P** — Replaces the author’s characterization with several invented actions and sensations.

## Case: fiction-b

### Edit P

**Preservation: 5/5**

No changes. All facts, voice, direct address, moral self-assessment, and rhetorical choices are preserved.

**Task success: 5/5**

The original contains no clear error or genuine line-level problem requiring correction. Returning it unchanged is the right decision.

**Latent original issue**

The funeral’s identity is left unstated, and readers may wonder whether it was the brother’s funeral. That is a productive opening ambiguity, not an error. Likewise, “I always skip that year” deliberately sets up the narrator’s present attempt to stop omitting it. P appropriately leaves both alone.

---

### Edit Q

**Preservation: 2/5**

Meaning and voice changes:

- “That’s the thing about my brother” becomes “My brother,” removing conversational voice and emphasis without improving clarity.
- Adds that the narrator specifically skipped the year at the funeral. This is compatible with “I always skip that year,” but unnecessarily resolves its application to that occasion.
- Adds that nobody at the funeral “needed” the omission as much as the narrator did. This invents a psychological claim about the narrator and a comparative claim about everyone present.
- The new funeral emphasis encourages a stronger inference that this funeral was especially connected to the brother, possibly his own, although it still does not say so outright.
- Removes “I’m telling you now,” weakening the protected direct relationship between narrator and reader.
- Removes the narrator’s judgment that skipping the year was “a kind of lie,” deleting important moral force.
- Removes “I’m tired of being the brother who only tells the funny half,” deleting both his present motivation and his self-definition.
- Consequently, the opening no longer clearly promises that this telling will include the omitted year.

The added sentence is not warranted by an error in the original.

**Task success: 0/5**

The edit replaces the opening’s purpose and moral premise with newly invented funeral psychology. It also weakens the transition into the story: after saying “I always skip that year,” the narrator gives no reason why this telling will be different.

**Latent original issue**

The unidentified funeral is a deliberate hook best left unresolved. Q makes it more narratively loaded without authorial authorization. It also creates a new structural problem by deleting the explanation of why the narrator is finally telling the fuller story.

---

### Edit R

**Preservation: 5/5**

No meaning, voice, factual, or structural changes.

**Task success: 5/5**

Correctly recognizes that the original needs no editing.

**Latent original issue**

The funeral ambiguity and the narrator’s delayed disclosure are intentional literary choices. R appropriately preserves them.

---

### Edit T

**Preservation: 5/5**

Unchanged; all protected elements remain intact.

**Task success: 5/5**

No intervention was needed, and none was made.

**Latent original issue**

T correctly leaves the funeral unidentified and preserves the narrator’s explicit reason for addressing the reader now.

Across these cases, I would trust **R** most, though not without reservations. R shows appropriate restraint in fiction-b and, in fiction-a, makes a narrow, non-inventive alteration. Its fiction-a deletion is still unacceptable because it removes the specific fear and causal motivation, but it is less risky across genres than P’s creative embellishment, Q’s willingness to invent a new psychological premise, or T’s broader deletion of characterization.
