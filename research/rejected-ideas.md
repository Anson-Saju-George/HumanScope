# Rejected Ideas Log

Common "AI humanizer" folklore and design temptations we deliberately did NOT adopt as HumanScope
rules, with the reason and any narrow exception. Prevents these from creeping back in. Evidence
mostly from the humanizeai blog's cited research + StoryScope's own scope.

| # | Idea | Decision | Reason | Narrow exception |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Ban em-dashes** | Rejected as a universal rule | Em-dash rate tracks *model family* far more than human-vs-AI, so it's a weak authorship signal: one non-peer-reviewed preprint (Freeburg 2026) reports GPT-4.1 10.62, Twain 10.13, Claude Opus 9.09, human-essay baseline 3.23, Llama 0.00 per 1k words (numbers relayed, not independently verified; they don't establish a *cause*). Banning strips a legitimate device. | Flag only when em-dashes *cluster* as the sole connector in a passage (blader "weak-alone") |
| 2 | **Banned-word list ("delve", "tapestry", "testament")** | Rejected | A population-level frequency signal is *weak per-document* (not proof for a single text). "AI vocab" reportedly rose in PubMed from 2020, *before* ChatGPT (Matsui 2025, relayed). Bans flatten voice and misfire. | May *notice* a word repeated unnaturally often, as a specificity prompt — never a blanket ban |
| 3 | **Optimize for AI-detector score / "% human"** | Rejected as an objective | Brief §10. Detectors are unreliable (61% false-positive on non-native writers, Liang 2023; <80% accuracy, 26% on paraphrase, Weber-Wulff 2023) and raw-text baselines already hit 99.7–99.9% — chasing them is detector evasion, which corrupts the goal. Two of the source videos do this; we reject the method while noting their structural corroboration. | None |
| 4 | **"Add typos / deliberately degrade grammar / de-format to look human"** | Rejected | Rejected because it **conflicts with the goal (better writing)** — NOT because published fiction proves simple language/fragments/plain formatting are inferior. Human ≠ defective AI (brief §4). | *Genuine* readability simplification and intentional plain style are fine and often good; the rejected thing is *deliberate degradation* |
| 5 | **"Randomly vary sentence length / burstiness"** | Rejected as a mechanical rule | A detector-era heuristic; varying length *for its own sake* is noise. Rhythm should follow meaning. | Fix genuinely monotonous rhythm when it exists |
| 6 | **Present invented anecdotes/specifics as real-world evidence, testimony, or citation** | Rejected (integrity line) | Passing fabricated material off as *real* is hallucination/deception. NB: this is scoped — writing *fiction* (invented characters/events/in-world detail) is fully allowed; L5 "specificity" for real-world claims must use real referents. | In fiction, invention is the task; user may also supply real anecdotes to weave in |
| 7 | **Treat all 30 StoryScope features as 30 independent edit rules** | Rejected | Produces rigid, checklist-driven writing (brief §17), and 3 categoricals are double-listed (→ 30 unique). *(NB: Table 8's redundancy is a predictive-ablation fact, not proof a 30-item checklist mechanically "double-counts" in editing.)* | Use features as *diagnostic evidence* under the 6 lenses, not a switch bank |
| 8 | **Apply fiction findings to all prose (emails, docs, reports)** | Rejected | Paper is fiction-only. Some humanward moves are wrong or reverse outside fiction (clear organization/answer-first structure are virtues in reports; *nonfiction does not uniformly reward chronology either*). Ungated transfer would wreck technical writing. | Domain-gated transfer, rated per feature × genre × purpose |
| 9 | **"Maximize difference from AI / maximize narrative rarity per text"** | Rejected | Rarity ≠ quality (a random incoherent combo is also rare), and rarity is a *task-dependent* proxy measured in a space built to separate sources — so it's not a valid per-text quality target (Codex #8; "circular" overstates it — it's confounded, not tautological). Diversity is a property of a body of work. | Per instance: ask if a more specific, coherent, purpose-fit choice serves *this* reader — no "non-default for its own sake" |
| 10 | **Giant NEVER-list style guide (no rhetorical questions, no headings, no triplets…)** | Rejected | Brief §17: negative mega-lists themselves produce rigid, lifeless writing. | Strong guidance only where research/craft justifies it, always context-sensitive |
| 11 | **Mechanically strip every Wikipedia "tell" (rule-of-three, negative parallelism, significance)** | Rejected | humanizeai: these are often *the job* in marketing/opinion; a full scrub "sands off the only parts that had a pulse." Detection ≠ style guide. | Flag as candidates; keep the ones doing real work (flag-don't-flatten) |
| 12 | **Second pass = "find more to cut"** | Rejected (reframed) | A subtraction-maximizing audit flattens voice. | Keep a second pass, but as a **restraint/regression check**: "did I over-edit, break meaning, or add fabrication?" |
| 13 | **Force an interview/questionnaire before every task** | Rejected as default | The "8 Skills" interview pattern is valuable but adds friction; forcing it on every rewrite is annoying. | Offer it *optionally* for WRITE / voice-matching, or when intent is genuinely ambiguous |

**Meta-principle behind every rejection:** a statistical difference between human and AI text is
not automatically a writing-quality target (brief §25). HumanScope changes prose only where a
specific, coherent, genre-appropriate choice is genuinely better — proven by reader preference,
never by a classifier.
