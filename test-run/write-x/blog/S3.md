# Why we moved from Jira to Linear

Last quarter, our 12-person engineering team moved from Jira to Linear. We'd been on Jira for a long time, and switching wasn't a small decision. Here's why we did it, what got better, and what we gave up.

## The problem was speed, in two senses

The first was plain responsiveness. Jira felt slow. Opening a ticket, changing its status or moving it across a board took long enough to break concentration. Any one delay was minor. Repeated dozens of times a day across twelve people, they added up to real friction.

The second kind of slowness was in our process. We were spending too long grooming tickets, and keeping the backlog in order had become a job of its own. Some of that was our own habits rather than the tool. But Jira's flexibility made it easy to keep adding fields, states and rules, and we had.

## Why Linear fit

Two things made Linear the right fit for us.

**It's keyboard-first.** Almost everything in Linear can be done without touching the mouse: creating an issue, assigning it, changing its status, jumping between views. For a team that spends most of the day in an editor and a terminal, that matters. Updating a ticket is quick enough that it no longer feels like an interruption.

**Cycles match how we already work.** We plan in short, fixed-length iterations. Linear's cycles are built around that rhythm. Work that doesn't finish rolls into the next cycle rather than needing to be manually shuffled around. Planning has become a quick check of what's in the next cycle instead of a long grooming meeting.

Linear is also more opinionated than Jira, and for us that turned out to be a feature. There are fewer ways to configure it, so there's less to maintain and less to argue about.

## What we lost

The switch did cost us something. We relied on some of Jira's reporting, and Linear doesn't give us all of it. Reports we used to pull up without thinking about it are now either unavailable or need extra work to reconstruct.

We decided the trade was worth making. The time we used to spend fighting the tool and grooming tickets was costing us more than the missing reports. But it's a real gap, and if your team leans heavily on Jira's reporting, it's the first thing we'd tell you to check before you switch.

## Where we've landed

A quarter in, the team spends less time managing work and more time doing it. Tickets are easier to keep up to date, and planning no longer means a long grooming session.

Linear isn't the right tool for every team. For a small engineering team that works in short cycles and lives on the keyboard, it has been a better fit than Jira was for us.
