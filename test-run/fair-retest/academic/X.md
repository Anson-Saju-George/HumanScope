A growing body of evidence indicates that sleep plays a central role in memory consolidation
(Rasch & Born, 2013). These effects may be modest, and they appear to depend on sleep stage,
particularly slow-wave sleep, although the underlying mechanisms are not fully understood
(Diekelmann & Born, 2010). In this study, we use a within-subjects design to test the hypothesis
that targeted memory reactivation during slow-wave sleep improves retention relative to a wake
control condition.
