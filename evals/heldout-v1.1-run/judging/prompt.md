## SOURCE TEXT
Anna came home at six and dropped her bag by the door. The mail was on the hall table where her neighbor had left it: a catalog, a water bill, and a thin envelope with no return address. She would remember, years later, that the envelope was blue, and that she had almost thrown it away with the catalog.

She put the kettle on and stood at the window. The rain had stopped. She wondered whether her sister would ever forgive her for missing their father's funeral, and she decided that tomorrow she would finally call the adoption agency whose letter was waiting, unopened, in the hall.

## RESPONSE A
# Continuity diagnosis

The scene has one real continuity fault: Anna knows what is in the letter before she opens it. The two problems below both come from that fault. Everything else holds together.

## 1. Anna knows the sender before she opens the envelope

**Evidence:** Paragraph 1 describes "a thin envelope with no return address." Paragraph 2 has Anna, still before dinner, planning to "call the adoption agency whose letter was waiting, unopened, in the hall." Paragraph 3 says: "She opened the envelope after dinner. It was from an adoption agency in Leeds."

**Intended effect:** Paragraph 3 is written as a discovery. She learns who sent the letter only when she opens it, and she has to read it twice before she understands.

**Failure:** The envelope has no return address and hasn't been opened, so Anna can't know an adoption agency sent it. Paragraph 2 also gives the reader that information early. By the time the reveal in paragraph 3 arrives, the reader already knows it, and it reads as a contradiction rather than a surprise.

**Suggested fix:** Take the agency out of Anna's thoughts in paragraph 2. For example, end the sentence at the funeral, or have her glance at "the blue envelope" without knowing what it is. The reveal then stays in paragraph 3.

## 2. "Finally call" suggests she already knew about the agency

**Evidence:** In paragraph 2 she decides she "would *finally* call the adoption agency." Paragraph 3 says "She read it twice before she understood that it was about her."

**Intended effect:** The letter should be news to her, and the realization that it concerns her should come slowly.

**Failure:** "Finally" means she has known about the agency for a while and has been putting off contacting it. That contradicts both the reveal and her slow understanding in paragraph 3. It also doesn't fit a letter that arrived today.

**Suggested fix:** If you apply fix 1, this problem goes away too. If you actually want an earlier history with the agency, make that the story's premise. Set it up before this scene and rewrite paragraph 3 so the letter confirms something she half-expected instead of revealing something new.

## 3. (Minor) She plans to call tomorrow, then opens the letter tonight

**Evidence:** Paragraph 2 says she "decided that tomorrow she would finally call." In paragraph 3 she opens the letter the same evening.

**Failure:** This is only a problem as long as problems 1 and 2 are in the text. A plan to call about a letter she hasn't opened, followed by opening it a few hours later, reads oddly. Once the agency reference comes out of paragraph 2, this goes away. No separate change is needed.

## Checked and consistent (no change suggested)

- The envelope's details are consistent. It's "thin" in paragraph 1 and later remembered as "blue," the flash-forward says she "almost threw it away with the catalog," and it waits on the hall table, which matches "in the hall."
- The order of events works: arrival at six, kettle and window, dinner, then the letter after dinner.
- The funeral and the sister are brought in without earlier setup. That's backstory, not a contradiction, and a parent's funeral fits alongside an adoption storyline.
