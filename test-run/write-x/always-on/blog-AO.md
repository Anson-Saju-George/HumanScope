# Why our engineering team moved from Jira to Linear

*By [author name], [role] · [publish date]*

Last quarter, our 12-person engineering team moved its day-to-day work off Jira and onto Linear. We didn't do it because Linear was new or because someone saw a good demo. We did it because the way we were tracking work had started to slow down the work itself. This post covers what pushed us to switch, what got better, and what we gave up.

## What wasn't working

Two things kept coming up.

**Jira felt slow.** Tools lag sometimes, and we could live with that. What wore us down was that every small action seemed to cost a few clicks and a page load: filing a bug, moving a ticket, checking what a teammate was working on. None of it took long by itself, but we did it dozens of times a day, and it added up to a tool people put off opening.

**Grooming took too long.** Our backlog sessions had turned into their own job. We spent more time tidying, re-estimating and re-sorting tickets than deciding what to build next. For a team our size, that was time we couldn't really spare.

## Why Linear fit

**It's built around the keyboard.** Most of what we do in Linear, like creating an issue, assigning it, changing its status or jumping to another view, can be done without reaching for the mouse. That sounds minor, but it changed how often people actually keep their issues up to date. When updating a ticket takes a couple of keystrokes, people do it as they go instead of saving it for standup.

**Cycles match how we already worked.** Linear's cycles are fixed, time-boxed stretches of work that roll unfinished issues into the next one. That was close to the rhythm we already had, so we didn't have to bend our process to fit the tool. [Optional: our cycle length and how we plan at the start of each one.]

Together, these meant less time managing tickets and less time in grooming. The backlog now gets looked at as part of planning each cycle instead of in a separate marathon meeting.

## Would we do it again?

Yes. For a team of 12 that wants to spend less time on process and more time shipping, the speed and the fit with our cycles were worth more to us than the reports we gave up. A larger team, or one whose stakeholders depend on detailed Jira reporting, might reasonably decide the other way.
