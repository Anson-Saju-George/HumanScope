Recent work has demonstrated that sleep plays a pivotal role in memory consolidation (Rasch & Born,
2013). These effects may be modest and appear to depend on sleep stage, particularly slow-wave sleep,
though the precise mechanisms remain incompletely understood (Diekelmann & Born, 2010). In this
study, we use a within-subjects design to test the hypothesis that targeted memory reactivation during
slow-wave sleep enhances retention relative to a wake control condition.
