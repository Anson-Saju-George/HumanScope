# Why We Moved From Jira to Linear

Last quarter our engineering team moved from Jira to Linear. There are twelve of us, and for a team that size, the tool you use to track work shapes the work more than you'd expect. This post covers why we switched, what got better, and what we gave up.

## The problem wasn't Jira. It was us using Jira.

Jira is a capable product. Large organizations run on it, and a lot of what felt heavy to us exists because those organizations need it. We just aren't one of them.

Two things kept coming up in our retros.

**It felt slow.** Opening a ticket, changing its status, finding the board you wanted: each of these took a beat longer than it should have. On its own that's nothing. Across a dozen engineers doing it dozens of times a day, it added up to friction people started working around. Updates got batched. Tickets fell out of date. The board stopped reflecting reality, which undercut the reason we had a board in the first place.

**Grooming took too long.** Our backlog refinement sessions kept growing. We spent real time filling in fields, reconciling custom workflows, and arguing about which issue type something should be. Some of that structure was left over from an earlier version of the team and nobody had cleaned it up. By the end of a session we had tidy tickets, and fewer people were looking forward to the next one.

We could have spent a quarter reconfiguring Jira. We decided to try something built closer to how we already work.

## What Linear got right for us

**It's keyboard-first.** Almost everything in Linear can be done without touching the mouse. You can create an issue, assign it, set priority, and move it along in a few keystrokes, and the app responds instantly. That sounds minor. In practice it's why people keep their issues current now: updating one takes less effort than putting it off.

**Cycles match our rhythm.** We already worked in roughly two-week chunks, and Linear's cycles map onto that directly. Unfinished work rolls into the next cycle automatically, so the start of a sprint no longer involves a manual cleanup. We can see what we committed to, what we finished, and what slipped, without anyone having to build that view by hand.

**Less to configure.** Linear is opinionated. It gives you a sensible default workflow and nudges you to stick with it. For a small team that's a feature. Grooming is now mostly about the work itself (what it is, how big it is, who's taking it) instead of the metadata around it. Our refinement sessions are noticeably shorter.

## What we lost

The biggest cost of the switch was reporting.

Over the years we had built a set of Jira dashboards and reports that some of us leaned on heavily, both for our own planning and for updates to people outside engineering. Linear has solid built-in insights for cycles and projects, but it doesn't match everything we had. Some of our custom views and historical reports had no direct equivalent, and some of the history didn't carry over in a useful form.

We handled this in a few ways:

- We went through which reports people actually opened and dropped the ones nobody missed. That turned out to be more of them than we thought.
- For the ones that mattered, we rebuilt what we could in Linear and are covering the rest with lighter-weight exports while we work out a longer-term answer.
- We were honest with the stakeholders who relied on those reports about what would change, and when.

It's a real tradeoff and we don't want to gloss over it. If your team depends on detailed cross-project reporting, weigh that carefully before switching.

## Would we do it again?

Yes. For a team our size, day-to-day speed and a planning process people don't dread matter more than the reporting we lost. The board is more accurate because updating it is easy, and we get back time we used to spend on grooming.

If you're a small team feeling the same drag, it's worth a trial. Just go in knowing which reports you can't live without, and have a plan for them before you migrate.

# Why we moved from Jira to Linear

Last quarter, our 12-person engineering team moved its work out of Jira and into Linear. We're writing up why, and what it cost us, for anyone weighing the same move.

## The problem wasn't Jira's features. It was the friction.

Jira can do almost anything, and that wasn't our complaint. Two things wore us down.

**It felt slow.** Opening a ticket, changing its status, finding the thing someone had just mentioned in standup: each step took a little longer than it should have. For a team our size those small waits add up, and they made people less inclined to keep the tracker accurate. A tracker nobody wants to update soon stops matching the work.

**Grooming took too long.** Our backlog sessions had become a chore. We spent more of that time tidying tickets, fixing fields, re-sorting, and cleaning up stale items, than deciding what to build next. The meeting was supposed to be about priorities, and the process had started to crowd them out.

## Why Linear fit

**It's built for the keyboard.** Most of what we do in a day (creating an issue, assigning it, changing status, jumping between views) can be done without touching the mouse. That sounds minor. In practice, updating an issue now takes about as long as thinking about it, so the tracker stays current without anyone having to nag.

**Cycles match how we already worked.** We were already planning in short, fixed blocks of time. Linear's cycles made that rhythm the default rather than something we had to configure around. Unfinished work rolls into the next cycle, and at the start of each one we look at what's actually in it instead of rebuilding a sprint from scratch.

Taken together, planning got lighter. We still groom the backlog, but it no longer eats the meeting.

## What we gave up

Reporting. Over the years we had come to rely on some of the reports Jira gave us, and Linear doesn't replace all of them. For a team focused on shipping day to day, that was a trade we were willing to make. Still, it is a real gap, and if your team or your leadership depends heavily on detailed tracker reports, look closely at what Linear offers before you switch.

## Would we do it again?

Yes. For a team of our size and working style, a faster tool with less process overhead has been worth more than the reports we lost. That calculation won't be the same for every team, and the reporting question is the one we'd tell others to answer first.
